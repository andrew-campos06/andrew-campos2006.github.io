    • Tesla Internship

    Mechanical design and validation work on drive-inverter hardware and manufacturing fixtures.

    RoleMechanical Design Engineering Intern
    DurationJune–September 2026
    LocationPalo Alto, California
    ← Back to Projects

    1. Overview

    I interned on Tesla’s Drive Inverter Design team in Palo Alto during Summer 2026, where I designed and validated a fault-response component for the Tesla Semi. My work focused on developing hardware that could perform reliably under demanding vehicle operating conditions while meeting manufacturing and assembly requirements. The project combined mechanical design, engineering calculations, simulation, and physical testing.

    2. Approach

    I approached the project by breaking down the component’s requirements and identifying potential failure modes. A key challenge was accounting for how different materials behaved across temperature changes, particularly how thermal expansion and stress relaxation could affect mechanical retention. I used calculations and simulation to evaluate design choices, then compared those predictions with physical test data to guide iteration. Throughout the process, I considered how the component would be manufactured and assembled, balancing prototype performance with the consistency needed for production.

    3. Results

    I designed and tested a fault-response component for the Tesla Semi, using engineering analysis and physical testing to evaluate its performance and inform design revisions. The work gave me experience connecting material behavior, mechanical tolerances, and assembly methods to component reliability. It also strengthened my ability to take ownership of a hardware problem and move from initial calculations to prototypes and validation.
